TIM-HF2 Trial (Telemedical Interventional Management in Heart Failure II)

Koehler F et al. | Lancet 2018;392:1047-57 | Germany | n=1,571

RCT

✓ Inclusion Criteria

  • • NYHA functional class II or III
  • • Hospital admission for worsening HF within 12 months before randomization
  • • LVEF ≤45%, OR LVEF >45% if treated with oral diuretics
  • • PHQ-9 depression score ≤9 (major depression excluded based on TIM-HF subgroup analysis)

Source: Koehler 2018, page 3

✗ Exclusion Criteria

  • • Major depression (PHQ-9 score >9)
  • • On haemodialysis
  • • Hospital admission within 7 days before randomization
  • • Left ventricular assist device (LVAD)
  • • Coronary revascularization or CRT implantation within 28 days
  • • Scheduled for revascularization, TAVI, MitraClip, or CRT within 3 months

Source: Koehler 2018, page 3

⚠️ Biomarker-Based Risk Stratification (Critical Detail)

Patients were categorized as LOW RISK or HIGH RISK using the combination of:

Two Biomarkers Used:

  1. NT-proBNP (N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide) - marker of myocardial stress and volume overload
  2. MR-proADM (mid-regional pro-adrenomedullin) - marker of vascular function and endothelial integrity
Sensitivity Level NT-proBNP Cutoff MR-proADM Cutoff Patients Excluded from RPM NPV
100% (Maximum Safety) ≥125.1 pg/mL ≥0.63 nmol/L 10.8% 100%
98% ≥145.4 pg/mL ≥0.72 nmol/L 16.8% 97.7%
95% (Recommended) ≥413.7 pg/mL ≥0.75 nmol/L 27.0% 97.1%

Interpretation: Patients with NT-proBNP <413.7 pg/mL AND MR-proADM <0.75 nmol/L are NOT recommended for RPM (low risk, unlikely to benefit). This excludes 27% of patients while maintaining 95% sensitivity for detecting those who would have ≥30 lost days/year.

Risk category re-evaluated every 3 months using MR-proADM results from follow-up visits.

Source: Möckel 2019 (TIM-HF2 Biomarker Substudy), pages 1, 7-8

📡 Remote Monitoring Scheme

Patient Daily Tasks (at fixed time each day):

  1. Measure body weight (Seca 861 scale)
  2. Measure blood pressure & heart rate (A&D UA767PBT)
  3. Record 2-min or streaming ECG (PhysioMem PM 1000)
  4. Measure peripheral SpO2 (Masimo finger clip)
  5. Rate self-health status (scale 1-5) via tablet

Technology & Transmission:

  • • Central device: Physio-Gate PG 1000 digital tablet
  • • Transmission: Automated via mobile network (VPN secured)
  • • Frequency: Daily at fixed time
  • • Emergency: Mobile phone for direct TMC contact
  • • Compliance: 97% were ≥70% compliant with daily transfers

Source: Koehler 2018, page 3

Protocol

✗ Exclusion Criteria

⚖️ STABLE vs UNSTABLE Protocol - Detailed Criteria

🟢 STABLE Protocol Criteria

All criteria must be met:

  1. On OMT for at least 1 month (based on ESC guidelines)
  2. Euvolaemic - well set on diuretics without dosage adjustments in past month
  3. No change in complaints and symptoms during past month
  4. Sufficient clinical knowledge of HF and early symptom detection
  5. Adequate self-management - seeks timely contact if complaints
Monitoring: Vitals + symptoms ≥1x/week
Goal: Long-term surveillance, prevent deterioration

🔴 UNSTABLE Protocol Criteria

Any of the following:

  1. Newly diagnosed with HF (HF de novo)
  2. Patient had been (re)admitted for HF
  3. Exacerbation requiring medication changes including diuretics
  4. Not yet on OMT
  5. Patient is symptomatic, anxious and/or uncertain
  6. Still needs help/guidance in early symptom detection
Monitoring: BP/HR/Weight Mon-Fri + weekly questionnaire
Goal: Achieve OMT rapidly, decongestion, stabilization

🔄 Transition Criteria:

  • Stable → Unstable: Symptom worsening, weight gain from decompensation, (re)admission
  • Unstable → Stable: Reached OMT + stable weight + no diuretic adjustments needed (sustained 2-4 weeks)

NT-proBNP is an important indicator - patient-specific levels strongly correlate with volume status.

Source: Jansen 2025, Table 3, pages 4-5

🏥 Medical Service Centre (MSC) - 4-Level Intervention Structure

Level 1

Technical Support

  • • Support onboarding
  • • App installation help
  • • Technical troubleshooting
Level 2

Monitoring Nurses

  • • Unstable: Active patient contact for treatment changes
  • • Stable: Monitoring role
  • • Phone calls every 7-14 days (unstable)
  • • Use standardized call scripts
Level 3

HF Nurse Specialists

  • • Expert guidance
  • • Titrate GDMT (RASi, BB, MRA, SGLT2i)
  • • Adjust diuretics
  • • Individualize cut-off values
  • • Decide protocol transitions
  • • Weekly supervision meetings

Cardiologists

  • • Complex situations only
  • • When complications arise
  • • Approve individualized thresholds

  1. Text message via app to guide patient response

  3. Nurse initiates call for in-depth symptom assessment

Important: NOT a 24/7 service - limited to weekdays during office hours. Patients contact HF clinic/ED outside hours.

Real-World

✓ Inclusion Criteria

👨‍⚕️ Intervention Protocol

Patient Journey:

  1. Enrollment: Physician assists patient to join
  2. Onboarding (within 48h): Nurse call - complete medical file, system instructions
  3. Weekly: Complete symptom questionnaire + weight monitoring
  4. Education (starts day 21): 6 themes, 18 questions/cycle, ~9 weeks
  5. Daily Challenge: 2 true/false questions + tip (twice weekly)

For Non-Digital Patients (Phone-Based):

  • • 3 phone sessions per semester
  • • Topics: alert signs, weight management, salt intake, physical activity, medication adherence
  • • Educational diagnosis included
  • • New cycle each semester

Staffing Growth:

2 part-time nurses (2018) → 5 (2019) → 15 (2020) → 28 nurses (2022)

Note: Does NOT use connected scales, NOT 24/7 - distinguishes from TIM-HF2 approach.

MAR-HF Titration Study

Ruiz-Bustillo S et al. | Eur J Cardiovasc Nurs 2025;24:1365-74 | Spain | n=210

Prospective Cohort

✓ Inclusion Criteria

  • • HFrEF diagnosis (LVEF <40% at diagnosis or exacerbation)
  • • Referred for HF stabilization and titration of prognostic drugs
  • • Considered good candidate for telemedicine-based follow-up
  • • Newly diagnosed HFrEF (61%) OR chronic HFrEF with exacerbation/recent LVEF decrease (39%)

Source: Ruiz-Bustillo 2025, page 3

✗ Exclusion Criteria

  • • Severe psychiatric illnesses
  • • Moderate-to-severe cognitive impairment without usual caregiver
  • • Institutionalized patients
  • • Inability to carry out adequate follow-up
  • • Complete language barrier with no translator available
  • • HF clearly due to fixable non-cardiac cause
  • • Life expectancy <1 year (except if due to HF itself)

Source: Ruiz-Bustillo 2025, page 3

👩‍⚕️ Nurse-Led GDMT Titration Protocol (6 Months)

Videoconference Protocol (Every 2 Weeks):

Nurses hold structured videoconferences with patients to titrate specific medications based on:

  1. Current doses already receiving
  2. Tolerance to current doses
  3. Hemodynamic data (BP, HR from daily monitoring)
  4. Laboratory results - especially renal function and potassium
  5. Treatment goals for each patient

Sequence repeated every 2 weeks until maximally tolerated doses of all 4 prognostic drugs achieved.

What Nurses Do:

  • • Daily monitoring of BP, HR, weight, symptoms
  • • Biweekly videoconferences for titration
  • • Detection of early HF exacerbation signs
  • • Routine laboratory monitoring
  • • Follow pre-specified titration algorithms
  • • Document reasons preventing full titration

Cardiologist Role:

  • • Support nurses with titration algorithms
  • • Final videoconference to review titration
  • • Review echocardiography results
  • • Update follow-up plan at end of intervention

Barriers Preventing Optimal Titration:

Beta-blockers: Low HR (70.2%)
MRA: Hypotension (45.5%), Hyperkalaemia (38.4%)
