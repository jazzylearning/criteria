TIM-HF2 Trial

RCT

Koehler F et al., Lancet 2018 | Germany | n=1,571

Inclusion Criteria
  • NYHA functional class II or III
  • Hospital admission for worsening HF within 12 months before randomization
  • LVEF ≤45%, OR LVEF >45% with oral diuretic treatment
Exclusion Criteria
  • Major depression (PHQ-9 score >9)
  • On haemodialysis
  • Hospital admission within 7 days before randomization
  • LVAD implanted
  • Coronary revascularization within 28 days
  • CRT implantation within 28 days
  • Scheduled procedures (revascularization, TAVI, MitraClip, CRT) within 3 months
Stable vs Unstable Classification

Method: Risk stratification using biomarkers

  • Low/High risk defined by MR-proADM values at baseline and follow-up
  • Risk category re-evaluated every 3 months
  • Biomarker thresholds combined with daily transmitted data
Biomarkers & Decompensation Indicators

Biomarkers Monitored:

  • NT-proBNP (baseline and 3-monthly follow-up)
  • MR-proADM (baseline and 3-monthly; used for risk stratification)

Vital Signs (Daily):

  • Body weight, Blood pressure, Heart rate
  • SpO2, ECG (heart rhythm analysis)
  • Self-rated health status (scale 1-5)
Remote Monitoring Scheme
FrequencyDaily (at fixed time)
Data TransmittedWeight, BP, HR, SpO2, ECG, Self-rated health
Technology3-channel ECG device (PhysioMem PM 1000), BP device (UA767PBT), Scales (Seca 861), SpO2 (Masimo), Digital tablet (Physio-Gate PG 1000), Mobile phone
TransmissionAutomated via mobile network (VPN secured)
Data ReviewTMC at Charité-Berlin (physicians + HF nurses)
Intervention Protocol

Who Delivered:

  • TMC staff: 4 physicians + 5 nurses (full-time daytime)
  • Night shift: 1 physician on-site + 1 on-call
  • Certified nurses for home installation

Interventions:

  • Initial HF education during system installation
  • Monthly structured telephone interviews
  • Medication changes (3,546 interventions total)
  • Diuretic adjustments, GDMT uptitration
  • Contact within 24h if data missing
  • 3-monthly reports to local physicians

Response Times:

  • Daily data review
  • Monthly minimum patient contact
  • 24/7 emergency availability

TIM-HF Trial

RCT

Koehler F et al., Circulation 2011 | Germany | n=710

Inclusion Criteria
  • Age ≥18 years
  • Stable, ambulatory patients with chronic HF
  • NYHA functional class II or III
  • LVEF ≤35%
  • For LVEF 26-35%: ≥1 HF hospitalization OR IV diuretics in past 24 months
  • For LVEF ≤25%: No prior hospitalization required
  • Optimally treated for HF per guidelines
Stable vs Unstable Classification

Study specifically enrolled stable, ambulatory CHF patients only. Stability defined by:

  • Ambulatory status maintained
  • NYHA class II-III
  • Optimal medical treatment
Biomarkers & Indicators
  • NT-proBNP: baseline 2478±3266 pg/mL
  • MR-proADM: 1.00±0.50 nmol/L
  • MR-proANP: 334±237 pmol/L
  • Daily: Body weight, BP, 3-lead ECG
Remote Monitoring Scheme
  • Devices: Wireless Bluetooth → PDA → Cell phone (EDGE)
  • Data: 3-lead ECG, BP, Weight (50g precision scale)
  • Frequency: Daily self-assessment
  • Centers: Two TMCs (Berlin + Stuttgart), 24/7, physician-led
  • Emergency: Home emergency call system included
Intervention Protocol

Who:

  • Physician-led TMC staff (24/7)
  • Two centers with shared electronic records

What:

  • Measurement verification
  • Patient consultation
  • Treatment initiation as needed
  • ≥3-monthly contact with local physician

INH Study (HeartNetCare-HF)

RCT

Angermann CE et al., Circ Heart Fail 2012 | Germany | n=715

Inclusion Criteria
  • Age ≥18 years
  • Hospitalized with decompensated HF signs/symptoms:
    • Dyspnea at rest or minimal exercise PLUS ≥1 of:
    • Raised JVP, peripheral edema, S3, pulmonary congestion
  • LVEF ≤40% at randomization
  • Disabled patients with family assistance eligible
Exclusion Criteria
  • Only new-onset structural heart disease
  • Logistic reasons (distant residence, no telephone)
  • Severe cognitive dysfunction/Alzheimer's/deafness
  • Severe noncardiac disorders
  • Insufficient German language
Patient Stratification

Intervention frequency individualized by NYHA class:

  • NYHA III-IV: Weekly or fortnightly contacts
  • NYHA I-II: Monthly contacts
Monitoring & Symptoms

Vital Signs:

  • Weight (electronic scales provided)
  • Blood pressure (gauges provided)
  • Heart rate

19-Item START Questionnaire:

  • Worsening HF indicators (edema, dyspnea, vertigo, fatigue)
  • Cardiac symptoms, medication adherence
  • Healthcare utilization, mood, general well-being
Intervention Protocol (Nurse-Coordinated)
Who 4 specialist nurses (hospital-based call center), weekly cardiologist supervision, bimonthly psychologist supervision
What
  • Telephone-based structured monitoring (avg 12.5 min/call)
  • Medication uptitration (60% achieved)
  • Diuretic self-adjustment teaching
  • Patient education (92%), drug education (84%)
  • Specialist care coordination
Frequency
  • Weekly during first month post-discharge
  • Then: NYHA III-IV = weekly/fortnightly; NYHA I-II = monthly
  • Average: 2.3 calls/patient-month
Escalation Nurse → Physician consultation → GP interaction (74% of patients) → Specialist referral (66%)

Tele-HF Trial

RCT

Chaudhry SI et al., NEJM 2010 | USA | n=1,653

Inclusion Criteria
  • Hospitalized for HF in previous 30 days
  • Enrolled at 33 cardiology practices (2006-2009)
  • Written informed consent
Exclusion Criteria
  • Long-term nursing home residence
  • Life expectancy <6 months (non-HF causes)
  • Unable to stand on scale
  • Severe cognitive impairment
  • Planned hospitalization for procedure
Remote Monitoring Scheme
  • System: Tel-Assurance (Pharos Innovations) - IVR telephone system
  • Data: Daily weight, symptoms, general health, HF symptoms
  • Depression screening: PHQ-2 every 30 days
  • Transmission: Manual entry via telephone keypad
  • Review: Site coordinators review weekdays (except holidays)
  • Alerts: 29,163 variances generated (median 21/patient)
Intervention Protocol
  • Who: Site coordinators + treating clinicians
  • What: Educational materials (HFSA), equipment demonstration, variance management
  • Response: Contact patients with variances; document management
  • Reminders: System-generated call after 2 days of non-use
  • Oversight: Yale Center review every 2-3 weeks

OSICAT Trial

RCT

Galinier M et al., Eur J Heart Fail 2020 | France | n=990

Inclusion Criteria
  • Age ≥18 years
  • Hospitalized for acute HF within 12 months
  • Access to landline telephone or GPRS network
  • French social security eligible
Exclusion Criteria
  • No echocardiographic evaluation
  • BNP <100 pg/mL or NT-proBNP <300 pg/mL
  • Life expectancy <12 months
  • Dialysis, heart transplant, LVAD
  • Need for inotropic treatment
  • Unable to use equipment
Remote Monitoring
  • Data: Daily body weight + 8 yes/no symptom questions
  • Devices: Electronic scales, symptom device
  • Transmission: Automated to secure server
  • Analysis: Expert system generates alerts automatically
  • Note: NO BP, HR, ECG, or SpO2 monitoring
Intervention Protocol
  • Who: HF-specialized nurses
  • Education: Information pack + telephone calls every 3 weeks
  • Alert Response: Nurse contacts patient → validates alert → advises GP contact if needed → 48h follow-up call
  • Follow-up: Calls at 15 days, 6, 12, 18 months

HerzMobil Tirol Programme

Observational

Poelzl G et al., Wien Klin Wochenschr 2021 | Austria | n=276 (HMT) + 276 (UC)

Inclusion Criteria
  • Age ≥18 years
  • Hospitalized with decompensated HF requiring IV diuretics
  • Any LVEF (HFrEF, HFmrEF, HFpEF included)
Exclusion Criteria
  • Charlson Comorbidity Index >6
  • Dementia
  • Lack of willingness to participate
Remote Monitoring Scheme
  • Data: BP, HR, Weight, Well-being, Drug intake
  • Frequency: Daily transmission
  • Technology: Configured smartphone + BP monitor + Scale
  • Analysis: Signal processing algorithms for automatic event detection
  • Thresholds: Individually defined and regularly adapted per patient
Intervention Protocol (3 months)
TeamHF cardiologist, specialty-trained HF nurses, network physicians (near patient's home), programme coordinator
HF Nurse RoleDaily data monitoring, patient education, home visits, telephone support, medication adjustment per physician instructions
Network PhysicianWeekly data review, evidence-based therapy optimization
Face-to-Face VisitsWeek 1, Week 4, Week 12
Home VisitImmediately post-discharge

SCAD Programme (France)

Observational

Sabatier R et al., Eur J Heart Fail 2022 | France (Normandy) | n=766

Inclusion Criteria
  • Hospitalized for HF (acute exacerbation, unplanned CV hospitalization with HF diagnosis, or planned HF follow-up)
  • HF diagnosis by cardiologist (NYHA criteria)
  • Agreed to participate in SCAD programme
  • Received therapeutic education during hospitalization
  • Telemonitored for ≥3 months
Patient Stratification by Use Level
  • Low users: <66.7% of anticipated sessions
  • Intermediate users: 66.7-87.5%
  • High users: ≥87.5% of sessions
Remote Monitoring Scheme

FridayFatigue/morale (1-10 VAS), treatment compliance
SaturdayEffort training

Technology: Tactile pad with secure internet portal

Alert System

Response times: 74.1% answered within 24h, 57.9% same day

Netherlands hTMS Protocol

Protocol

Jansen C et al., ESC Heart Fail 2025 | Netherlands | 7 hospitals

Inclusion Criteria
  • Newly diagnosed with HF
  • Age >18 years
  • Smartphone/tablet for Thuismeten app
  • Sufficient digital literacy (patient or caregiver)
  • Dutch language proficient
  • Access to scales and BP monitor
Stable vs Unstable Protocol

UNSTABLE Protocol Criteria:

  • Newly diagnosed HF (de novo)
  • Recent HF (re)admission
  • HF exacerbation requiring medication changes
  • Not yet on OMT
  • Symptomatic, anxious, uncertain
  • Needs guidance on symptom detection

STABLE Protocol Criteria:

  • On OMT for ≥1 month
  • Euvolaemic, stable diuretics ≥1 month
  • No symptom changes ≥1 month
  • Sufficient HF knowledge
  • Adequate self-management

MAR-HF Titration Study

Prospective Cohort

Ruiz-Bustillo S et al., Eur J Cardiovasc Nurs 2025 | Spain | n=210

Inclusion Criteria
  • HFrEF (LVEF <40% at diagnosis or exacerbation)
  • Referred for HF stabilization and drug titration
  • Good candidates for telemedicine follow-up
  • Newly diagnosed HFrEF (61%) or chronic with exacerbation (39%)
Exclusion Criteria
  • Severe psychiatric illness
  • Moderate-severe cognitive impairment (without caregiver)
  • Institutionalized
  • Inability to follow-up
  • Complete language barrier
  • HF due to fixable non-cardiac cause
  • Life expectancy <1 year (except if due to HF itself)
Remote Monitoring (Daily)
  • Data: BP, HR, Weight, HF symptoms (every morning)
  • Devices: Touchscreen tablet (4G), BP monitor, Scale
  • Biomarkers: NT-proBNP, eGFR, potassium at baseline and end
  • Review: Nurses monitor daily; cardiologists provide support
Nurse-Led Titration Protocol (6 months)

Videoconferences every 2 weeks covering:

  • Current doses and tolerance
  • Hemodynamic data
  • Lab results (renal function, potassium)
  • Treatment goals

Median titration times achieved:

  • Beta-blockers: 45 days
  • MRA: 65 days
  • ARNI: 69 days
  • All four medications: 99 days

Outcome: Quadruple therapy ↑ from 63% to 75%; LVEF ↑ from 31% to 45%

TELESAT-HF Study

Real-World Evidence

Girerd N et al., Eur J Heart Fail 2025 | France | n=35,000+

Inclusion Criteria
  • Adults in France hospitalized for HF (Aug 2018 - Dec 2021)
  • HF hospitalization (strict or expanded ICD-10 definition)
  • COVID period modification: HF treatment + NYHA ≥II + BNP >100 or NT-proBNP >1000 pg/mL
Alert Algorithm (Satelia® Cardio)
  • Green: Score <12 (routine)
  • Orange alert: Score ≥12
  • Red alert: Score ≥21
  • NPV: 99% negative predictive value
  • Frequency auto-tailored to patient risk profile
Remote Monitoring
  • Data: Weekly HF symptom questionnaire, regular weight
  • Technology: Satelia® Cardio web app or Télesphore® phone platform
  • Note: NO connected scales, NO oximetry, NOT 24/7
  • Education: 6 themes, 18 questions/cycle, ~9 weeks
  • Daily Challenge: 2 true/false questions + tip (twice weekly)
Intervention Protocol
  • Onboarding: Nurse phone call within 48h of inclusion
  • Digital patients: Self-service via web app
  • Non-digital patients: 3 phone sessions/semester
  • Alert management: Many managed via phone/telemedicine (diuretic increase, NT-proBNP testing)
  • Staffing: 2 part-time nurses (2018) → 28 nurses (2022)

Providence/Cadence RPC Program

Real-World

Feldman DI et al., NEJM Catalyst 2025 | USA | n=2,500+

Eligibility
  • Established Providence patients, Medicare beneficiaries
  • Diagnosis: Hypertension, Heart Failure (any EF), and/or Type 2 DM
  • Proprietary algorithm for enrollment
Remote Monitoring

By Condition:

  • HTN: Blood pressure
  • HF: BP, HR, Weight
  • DM: Blood glucose

Devices: Cellular-enabled (automatic transmission)

Compliance: 61-76% meeting ≥16 vitals/month at 6 months

Review: RN team 24/7/365; NPs address clinical alerts

Alert Volume
  • HTN: 9,153 high-acuity alerts (1,098 patients)
  • HF: 11,176 alerts (481 patients)
  • DM: 251 alerts (74 patients)
  • Escalation rate: <1% to longitudinal clinicians
Intervention (AI-Assisted)
  • Team: NP-led clinical team + RN 24/7 triage + non-clinical support
  • AI-assisted algorithms: Guideline-based tasks generated automatically
  • GDMT: Goal >50% target dose for each medication class
  • HF medications: RAASi: 75% at 6mo; BB: 95%; MRA: 59%; SGLT2i: 77%
  • Notes: All clinical notes published to Providence EHR

Algorithm Response Patterns

Kurtz et al. Point-Based System:

  • 0 points (stable): Repeat measurements in 1 week
  • 1 point (minor worsening): Repeat in 3 days
  • 2 points (suspected deterioration): Medical visit, increase phone contacts

JACC Scientific Statement

Guideline

Stevenson LW et al., JACC 2023 | Expert Consensus

Monitoring Intent Categories
CategoryRisk LevelIntervention Frequency
Routine Monitoring ~20-30% HF event risk at 6mo Initially weekly; decrease to monthly when stable
Long-Term Surveillance ~10% HF event risk at 6mo Rare until repeated exceptions
"Hospital at Home" ~50% readmission at 6mo ≥2x daily during IV diuretics
Key Decompensation Indicators
  • PA pressures: Rise ~4 weeks before HFH (HFrEF), ~3 weeks (HFpEF)
  • PAD target: 8-22 mmHg; >18 mmHg = higher risk
  • Thoracic impedance: ↓12% avg, ~18 days before HFH
  • ReDS lung water: Normal 28%; >35% predicts HFH
  • Weight: <2 lb increase typical before HFH (10-20% sensitivity)
  • HR: Nocturnal ↑ many days before HFH
  • HeartLogic alert: 10× risk when in alert zone
Response Time Recommendations
SettingResponse Time
Direct patient loop (app algorithm)Immediate personalized instructions
Routine monitoringTherapy change within 24-72 hours
SurveillanceArrange clinical evaluation when triggered
Hospital at home6-12 hours; 24/7 ongoing adjustment

Germany 20-Year Telemonitoring Journey

Review

Spethmann S et al., JMIR 2024 | Narrative Review

G-BA Standard Care Criteria (Germany)
  • NYHA II-III with LVEF <40%
  • Implanted cardiac device (ICD/CRT-P/D) OR HF hospitalization in past year
  • Present treatment per current guidelines (GDMT)
  • No factors compromising data transfer or self-management
G-BA Response Time Requirements
  • Data evaluation: ≥5 days/week
  • Severely ill patients: Daily evaluation ("intensified monitoring")
  • Alert processing: Must not exceed 24 hours
  • Transmission to primary physician: Maximum 48 hours
